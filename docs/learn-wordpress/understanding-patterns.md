Patterns are the fastest way to build in WordPress: pre-designed arrangements of blocks — a hero section, a pricing table, a testimonial row, even a full page — that you insert with one click and then customize like any other content. They're the core of the Ollie workflow, so it's worth understanding exactly how they behave.

## What a Pattern Is

A pattern is a saved collection of blocks. When you insert one, it becomes *your* blocks — regular, fully editable content on that page, with no link back to the original. Change the text, swap the images, restyle anything. Insert the same pattern on ten pages and each copy is independent.

Because Ollie's patterns are built against your theme's design tokens, they inherit your [Global Styles](/docs/design/global-styles/) automatically — every pattern arrives already matching your fonts and colors.

## Patterns vs. Synced Patterns vs. Templates

Three ideas that sound similar but behave very differently:

- **Patterns** — a starting design. Once inserted, each copy is independent. Use them for page sections you'll customize per page.
- **Synced patterns** — one shared source. Edit a synced pattern once and it updates *everywhere* it appears. Use them for content that must stay identical site-wide: a call-to-action banner, a promo strip, contact details.
- **Templates** — the layout layer *around* your content: how single posts, archives, and pages are structured. Patterns fill content in; templates frame it.

The mental model: **synced = one source of truth, unsynced = a smart starting point.** Reach for synced when a future edit must propagate; reach for regular patterns everywhere else.

## Using Patterns in Ollie

Open the block inserter (**+**) and switch to the **Patterns** tab — Ollie ships more than 50 designs organized by category (heroes, features, pricing, testimonials, cards, and more). Click to preview, click to insert, customize from there.

With Ollie Pro, the [Pattern Library](/docs/patterns/) goes much further: hundreds of cloud patterns with live previews, device testing, favorites, and search — see [Pattern Library Tips](/docs/build/pattern-library-tips/) for the power features.
